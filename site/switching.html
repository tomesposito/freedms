Switching systems without losing your lot

Dealers don’t stay with a DMS they hate out of loyalty. They stay because moving sounds worse. The inventory list, the customer names, the half-finished deals, all sitting in the old system, and a vendor who may or may not hand your own data back politely. This page is the honest version of the move to freeDMS. What comes across, what needs a decision from you, and what does not come at all. Read the third list first if you are in a hurry. It is the one most vendors skip.

The sequence, before the detail:

  1. Audit what you actually have in the old system.
  2. Export what it will give you, while you are still a paying customer in good standing.
  3. Decide what is worth carrying and what is dead weight.
  4. Load the inventory into freeDMS and check the numbers.
  5. Run both systems side by side for a week or two.
  6. Cut over on a quiet day, not the last Saturday of the month.

Nothing on that list is a feature. It is the order that keeps a switch from becoming a lost weekend.

What moves cleanly:

Your current inventory. freeDMS imports vehicles from a CSV file, and the import is built for exactly this: a list that came out of another system.

  • You preview before anything is written. The preview shows every row as valid, an error with the reason, or a duplicate, matched on VIN or stock number. Nothing loads until you confirm.
  • The common column names are accepted as they come: "stock #," "asking price," "our cost," and their cousins map to the right fields. Year, make, model, trim, VIN, mileage, cost, price, status, date stocked, and location all come across.
  • If your file is thin on year, make, and model, a VIN decode can fill those in during the preview.
  • Files run up to a thousand rows at a time. A bigger lot goes in batches, and duplicates are skipped, not loaded twice.
  • The import keeps a history inside the product: when it ran, how many loaded, how many were skipped. That record is part of how you prove the move later.

New leads also keep arriving during a switch. The lead intake takes the standard ADF format the listing sites send, so the flow of new customers does not have to pause while you change systems.

What needs a decision:

This is the part a one-click promise hides. Some of your data has no bulk path into freeDMS, and pretending otherwise would set you up.

  • Customers and leads. There is no customer or lead import. Your active ups, the people mid-conversation, get re-entered by hand or as they come back in. That sounds bad until you count them: on most lots the live list is twenty names, not two thousand. The two thousand are dead leads, and a switch is the cheapest chance you will ever get to leave them behind. Rule on it: keep, or drop.
  • Deals in progress. A deal half-worked in the old system does not transfer. Finish it there, or start it fresh here. Deals that span the cutover are where numbers go to get lost, so pick one side per deal.
  • Recon detail. The import carries one cost number per car. If your old system tracks recon as separate line items, either fold the recon total into the car's cost before you export, or re-enter the lines on the cars still in the shop. Cars already sold do not care. Cars in recon do.
  • Sold history. Your past deals do not load as deals. Export the sales reports from the old system and keep them as your record. freeDMS history starts the day you do.

What does not move:

  • Your loan book, if you carry paper. freeDMS has no payment schedules, no balances, no collections. A buy here pay here lot switching its front end keeps its loan system where it is. The BHPH page on this site has the full split.
  • Your accounting history. The general ledger here starts fresh. Your bookkeeper keeps the old system, or its exports, for prior periods. That is normal in a switch and it is still a cost, so count it.
  • Deal jackets and documents from the old system. The paper trail of closed deals stays in the old system or in your files. New deals build their jackets here.
  • Vehicle photos. The CSV carries the car's facts, not its pictures. Photos get re-shot or re-uploaded car by car.
  • Follow-up notes and call history on customers. What your people remember moves. What was typed into the old CRM does not.

How the numbers get proven:

A switch you cannot check is a switch you are trusting. Check this one in counts and dollars, before and after.

  • Before you load: run the inventory report in your old system. Write down the count of cars, the total cost in them, and the total asking price. Three numbers.
  • At the load: the import preview tells you valid rows, errors, and duplicates before you confirm, and the result after. Errors get fixed in the file and re-run; duplicates are skipped by VIN or stock number, so a re-run cannot double your lot.
  • After you load: the inventory report here shows every car with cost, recon total, and total cost, with its age in days. Count the cars. Add the cost column. Compare against the three numbers you wrote down. They should match, or you should know exactly which cars explain the difference.
  • During the parallel run: sell out of one system per deal, and reconcile the week: cars sold, deals closed, leads worked. When a full week ties out in both places, the old system is a reporting archive, not a crutch. That is the day you cut over.

Getting your data out of the old system:

The hard part of this move is rarely our side. It is the exit from where you are.

Some dealers find an export button. Some find a fee for their own records, a contract with months left on it, or a price for help that was never written down until they asked to leave. We are not going to quote you another vendor's current terms; they change, and the only copy that counts is your contract. Read it before you give notice. Ask for the export while you are a customer, not after you have cancelled, and ask what the file contains, column by column, against the list in the "moves cleanly" section above. If the file has VINs, costs, prices, and dates stocked, your inventory move is an afternoon.

What we do not have:

No migration wizard. No converter that speaks Frazer or DealerCenter or anyone else's format; the CSV import is generic, and your export file may need its columns tidied before it loads.

No done-for-you move to sell you. Dealers join the pilot a few at a time, and questions get answered by a person, but the loading is yours, with the preview doing the checking. Anyone who promises you a switch with no work in it is planning to do the deciding for you, quietly, and you will find out later what they dropped.
